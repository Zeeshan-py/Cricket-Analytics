Batting strike rate measures scoring speed. It tells you how many runs a batter scores per 100 balls faced.

The formula is:

| Metric | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Strike rate | Runs scored / Balls faced x 100 |

If a batter scores 75 from 60 balls, the strike rate is 125.00.

## Why strike rate matters

Modern cricket often depends on scoring tempo. Strike rate helps explain how quickly a batter changes the match state.

This is especially important in limited-overs cricket:

- In T20 cricket, a high strike rate can be more valuable than a long innings.
- In ODI cricket, strike rate must be balanced with innings construction.
- In Test cricket, strike rate can still matter, but survival and match situation often carry more weight.

## Strike rate and batting role

Strike rate should be read with the batter's role.

Openers may face the new ball and fielding restrictions. Middle-order players may rebuild after wickets. Finishers may face only a few balls and need immediate acceleration.

A lower strike rate is not always poor, and a high strike rate is not always enough. The context matters.

## Average and strike rate together

Average tells you how often a batter is dismissed. Strike rate tells you how fast they score.

Together, they give a better picture:

- High average, high strike rate: high-impact scoring
- High average, low strike rate: reliable but slower scoring
- Low average, high strike rate: volatile attacking role
- Low average, low strike rate: limited output

## Summary

Strike rate is runs per 100 balls. It is one of the most important batting metrics in limited-overs cricket, but it should always be interpreted through match format, batting role, and game situation.
