Bowling economy rate measures how many runs a bowler concedes per over. It is a pressure metric as much as a scoring metric.

The formula is:

| Metric | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Economy rate | Runs conceded / Overs bowled |

If a bowler concedes 36 runs in 6 overs, the economy rate is 6.00.

## Why economy rate matters

Economy rate shows how well a bowler controls scoring. In limited-overs cricket, control can be just as valuable as wickets because every over has a fixed opportunity cost.

A bowler who takes fewer wickets but gives away very few runs may still have a major impact.

## Economy rate vs bowling average

Bowling average measures runs conceded per wicket. Economy rate measures runs conceded per over.

They answer different questions:

- Economy rate: how expensive is the bowler?
- Bowling average: how many runs does the bowler concede for each wicket?
- Bowling strike rate: how often does the bowler take a wicket?

## Format context

Economy rates vary by format.

In Test cricket, a low economy rate can build pressure over long spells. In ODI cricket, economy often reflects middle-over control. In T20 cricket, even small differences can decide a match.

## Summary

Bowling economy rate is runs conceded per over. It is essential for understanding control, pressure, and limited-overs value, but it should be read alongside wickets, role, phase, and match format.
