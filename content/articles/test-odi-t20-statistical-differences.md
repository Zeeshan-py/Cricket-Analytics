Cricket statistics change meaning across formats. A good number in Test cricket may be average in T20 cricket, and a useful T20 skill may not translate neatly to multi-day cricket.

## Test cricket

Test cricket rewards durability, patience, and repeatable skill over long periods.

Important indicators include:

- Batting average
- Balls faced
- Bowling average
- Long spells
- Match result context

Strike rate can matter, but it is usually read alongside match situation.

## ODI cricket

ODI cricket sits between Test and T20. Players must balance innings building with scoring speed.

Useful ODI measures include:

- Runs and average
- Strike rate
- Economy rate
- Middle-over control
- Wickets in key phases

ODI statistics often require phase context: powerplay, middle overs, and death overs can be very different.

## T20 cricket

T20 cricket compresses opportunity. A player may face only a few balls or bowl only a short spell.

Important T20 indicators include:

- Batting strike rate
- Boundary rate
- Economy rate
- Dot-ball pressure
- Wickets without excessive runs

Small samples can mislead, so T20 numbers need careful handling.

## Summary

Never compare cricket numbers without asking which format produced them. Test, ODI, and T20 cricket reward different skills, and the best analysis respects those differences.
