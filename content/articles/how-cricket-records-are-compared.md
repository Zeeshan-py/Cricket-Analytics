Cricket records are powerful because they make performance easy to remember. Highest score, most runs, best bowling figures, most wickets: each record creates a simple headline.

But record comparison needs care.

## Define the record clearly

A record should specify:

- Format
- Competition or dataset
- Time period
- Qualification rules
- Whether it is match, innings, career, team, or tournament based

Without those boundaries, record claims can become misleading.

## Dataset coverage matters

Cricket Atlas currently displays records from the verified imported dataset only. These are not claims about all cricket history.

The [records page](/records) labels current-dataset records accordingly.

## Era and format context

Records from different eras can be hard to compare. Playing conditions, scoring rates, fielding standards, and scheduling patterns change over time.

Format also matters. A T20 strike-rate record should not be judged by the same standard as a Test batting average.

## Team and venue context

Venue size, pitch type, weather, and opposition quality can all affect records. A strong analysis asks what conditions produced the number.

## Summary

Records are useful entry points, but they need clear boundaries. Good record pages explain what is being measured, what data is included, and what context readers should know.
